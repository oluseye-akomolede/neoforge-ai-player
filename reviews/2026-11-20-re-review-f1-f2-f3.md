## F-4 — the spec's cap scenario cannot fire (new; the lead's own question)

The builder's finding is **correct**: with `COOLDOWN_SECONDS * MAX_RESULTS_PER_HOUR
== HOUR_SECONDS` exactly (900 × 4 = 3600), the cooldown alone holds the rate to
4/hour and the hour-cap can only bite on the exact boundary. I replayed
`ResultSlot` against the real constants; ten completions spaced 900 s apart all get
a call and nothing is refused, because the first `record_call()` prunes itself from
`_recent_calls()` by the time the fifth is asked.

**CORRECTION (lead, 23:59Z; verified here).** My "10 calls in 2.5 hours is more
than double 4/hour" was wrong, and I withdraw it. Ten calls at 900 s gaps span
8100 s and **no rolling 3600 s window ever holds more than 4** (0/900/1800/2700;
the next at 3600 falls outside the window opened at 0). That is exactly the rate
the cooldown promises — the guard is correct.

My "899 s gap → 5 calls" was also a measurement error, not a defect. 899 s
arrivals are refused by the cooldown, and the *refused result stays in the slot*,
so the next two arrivals overwrite it and the call that does land is 1798 s after
the last one — 13 s past the cooldown, not early. Replaying without replacing the
waiting result (10 s arrivals) reproduces the lead's figure exactly: calls at
0/900/1800/2700/3600, four in every window. No rolling-window violation exists at
either gap.

The cooldown boundary is correct and strict: `result_delivery.py:77-78`
`if recent and (self._clock() - max(recent)) < self._cooldown: return False,
"cooldown"` — allowed at exactly 900 s, refused at 899.999 s. Correct.

| gap between completions | calls | max in any 3600 s window |
|---|---|---|
| 300 s | 4 | 4 |
| 899 s | 5 | 3 |
| 900 s | 10 | 4 |

So the **cooldown is the binding limit, and it holds the promised rate.** The cap
is a backstop that cannot fire on this path. `design.md:463` frames the cap as
"the difference between 4 planner calls and 59" — that describes the
59-ticks-per-hour runaway, not this path, which is the wording F-4 asks to fix.

Constants stay, per your decision. The problem is the **spec**, which currently
promises the cap as the acceptance test:

- `spec.md:121-129` — *"GIVEN ten distinct scripts… WHEN they are bound and run back
  to back within one hour … THEN at most 4 results MUST reach the planner"*. Back to
  back means no 900 s gaps, so the cooldown refuses nine of ten and the assertion
  passes **on the cooldown, not the cap** — the cap could be deleted and it stays
  green (I confirmed: M2' reds a unit test, not a spec-level one).
- `spec.md:131` then says the scenario is *"also what proves the cooldown is per bot
  rather than per script"* — it cannot prove that, because ten stored results in one
  pass collapse to one (rule 2's overwrite), so the tenth script's identity is gone
  before the planner sees it.
- No test asserts the scenario at spec level (`grep` for ten-scripts/fifth-script
  in `agent/test_*.py` → zero hits); it is covered by `PlannerForPassHonoursTheCapsTest`,
  which lifts the cooldown (`slot._cooldown = 0`) — a **test-only configuration**.
  The check is honest and self-documenting, but it means the cap is proven reachable
  only in a state production never enters.

**What I'd ask for:** reword `spec.md:121-134` to separate the two claims — the
cooldown is the binding rate limit (4/900 s), the cap is a backstop reachable only
if the cooldown is shortened — and state that ordering not as "ten scripts back to
back" (which the cooldown alone satisfies) but as "four calls inside one hour
refuse the fifth". If the intent really is that ten *scripts*, not ten *results*,
must be held off, the slot's single-entry overwrite is the thing that breaks it and
that needs its own delta. This does not block the code; it blocks the spec sentence
being cited later as proof.

## Why the new cap test is honest but thin

`test_the_four_a_hour_cap_turns_it_red` asserts the exact relationship
`COOLDOWN_SECONDS * MAX_RESULTS_PER_HOUR == HOUR_SECONDS` up front and says the
reachability reasoning depends on it. That is good practice — it will fail loudly if
a constant moves. But it means the test greens only while the cap is provably dead
in production. Keep it; pair it with the spec wording above so nothing downstream
reads the cap as the binding guard.
